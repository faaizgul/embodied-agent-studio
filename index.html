Where to get a body
  • VRoid Studio (free, Windows and macOS): design an anime-style character and use Export as VRM. Full face, blinking and lip movement. vroid.com/en/studio
  • VRoid Hub: ready-made VRM avatars by other people. Each one has its own licence; check it allows your use.
  • MetaPerson Creator: a realistic avatar from a selfie. The first avatar is free. Using a real face raises consent questions: whose face is it, and who agreed?
  • MakeHuman for Blender (MPFB): free, open-source realistic humans. Its viseme pack adds the mouth shapes this studio uses. Export as .glb.
  • Your own model in Blender, human or not: add a rig and convert it with the free VRM Add-on for Blender, or export a .glb with shape keys named like ARKit (jawOpen, eyeBlinkLeft).
  • Free character packs (Quaternius, Kenney, KayKit, 100 Avatars): good bodies, but most have no face shapes, so the mouth will not move. That is a design limit worth discussing.
Design questions for the body

The uncanny valley. In 1970 the roboticist Masahiro Mori proposed that as a robot looks more human, people feel more affinity for it, until it is almost but not quite human. At that point affinity drops into eeriness: the uncanny valley. He noted that movement makes the effect stronger, and advised designers to aim for moderate human likeness, or even a deliberately non-human look, rather than risk the valley.

Mori, M. (1970). Bukimi no tani [The uncanny valley]. Energy, 7(4), 33–35.

Mori, M., MacDorman, K. F., & Kageki, N. (2012). The uncanny valley [From the field]. IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine, 19(2), 98–100. doi.org/10.1109/MRA.2012.2192811

  • Try the robot, the mascot and your own avatar. Where does each one sit on Mori's curve? Which feels most trustworthy, and why?
  • Human, animal, object or abstract? Realistic or stylised? What does each choice promise about how smart the agent is?
  • Does its look match its job and the people it serves? What does its posture say before it speaks?
  • If it is based on a real person's face, who agreed to that, and what could go wrong?

Design questions for the name
  • The name is the first thing people learn about the agent. What does it signal: human or machine, gender, age, region, formality?
  • Does it suit the culture and language of the people it serves? Is it easy for them to say?
  • A human name can raise expectations of human understanding. Does your agent live up to that, or would a non-human name set fairer expectations?
  • In "always listening" mode the agent wakes up when it hears its name. Pick one that speech recognition hears clearly, and that people will not say by accident.

          Design questions for the voice
          

            
    
              
  • Does the voice match the body and the name? What accent and pace suit the people it serves?
    • 
              
  • Should it sound human at all, or clearly synthetic? A very human voice on a robot body can feel uncanny too.
    • 
              
  • How does it show that it is listening, or thinking? People need to know when the microphone is on.
    • 
              
  • Press to talk gives people control. Always listening feels more natural, but the microphone never rests. Which is right for your setting?

          How the demo file works
          

            
What is inside: the whole studio, your character sheet, voice settings and avatar, in one web page. It opens straight in presentation view.

            
What is not inside: your API key. Visitors press Add AI key and paste their own free key, or use the script (rehearsal mode). It also needs an internet connection.

            
To share it as a link:

            
    
              
  1. Rename the file to index.html.
    2. 
              
  2. Create a free public GitHub repository and upload the file. GitHub's website accepts files up to 25 MB.
    3. 
              
  3. In the repository, open Settings, then Pages. Choose Deploy from a branch, branch main, folder / (root), and save.
    4. 
              
  4. After a minute or two the link appears at the top of the Pages settings. Open it on a phone to test.
    5. 
            

            
Larger than 25 MB? Use Netlify Drop (drag the file onto app.netlify.com/drop), or export your VRoid avatar with smaller textures.
